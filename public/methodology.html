Methodology version 1.0.0-alpha.1

An index that shows its workings.

The UK Stability Monitor measures observable systemic pressure on the United Kingdom's economic, household, institutional, public-service and environmental systems. It is not a forecast of state failure, a political approval score, or a substitute for the underlying evidence.

No headline is published. Four of the ten indicators have working collectors, covering 40% of fixed weight. The publication gates require 90%. Until they are met, the site shows what has been measured and what has not, and withholds the single number.

1. Four outputs, kept apart

The monitor never reduces itself to one figure. It publishes four things, and the interface is required to show them together.

Structural pressureFixed-weight sum of measured indicator pressure, 0–100.
Acute overlayReviewed, decaying disruption contribution, capped at 8 points.
Headline scoremin(100, structural + acute) — published only when the gates pass.
ConfidenceEvidence health, 0–100. Not a statistical confidence interval.

2. Weights are fixed and never renormalised

This is the central commitment of version 1, and the thing most composite indices get wrong. When an indicator has no data, its weight is not redistributed across the others:

structural pressure = Σ(weighti × pressurei)

The sum is not divided by the weight of available indicators. If it were, four indicators could be rescaled to look like a complete picture of the country, and a gap in the evidence would silently become a claim about it. Instead, missing evidence lowers coverage and confidence, and the site reports the range a complete score could occupy:

range = [ measured , measured + (missing weight × 100) ]

4. Transformations

Each raw value is mapped onto a 0–100 pressure scale by a published piecewise-linear curve: 0 is unusually low pressure, 50 is materially elevated, 100 is an extreme observed or policy-relevant condition. Curves are frozen within a version — they never recalibrate themselves as data arrives, because a bad year must not be allowed to quietly redefine "normal". Every breakpoint and its rationale is published at /api/v1/methodology.

Inflation is scored two-sided

Sustained deflation is destabilising too, so the inflation curve rises on both sides of the 2% target rather than only above it. It rises less steeply on the deflation side, because debt-deflation dynamics reach households more slowly than a price shock does. The trough is scored at 5 rather than 0: on-target inflation means prices are not currently adding to pressure, not that the cost of living is painless.

Counts are normalised by a versioned denominator

Working days lost is a count, so it is divided by an employment denominator (MGRZ) to give days lost per 1,000 employed people per month. The denominator's own reference period, publication date and evidence hash are recorded against every derived observation, so a later revision to employment cannot silently rewrite a published rate.

5. Confidence and freshness

confidence = Σ(weighti × qualityi × freshnessi × coveragei)

Because weights stay fixed, a missing indicator contributes zero confidence. Quality reflects the statistic's status, from accredited official statistics down to manually entered values. Coverage is the share of the UK population a source actually describes — England-only evidence is multiplied by roughly 0.845 and flagged, so it can never quietly stand in for the UK.

Freshness is judged against the source's own announced next release date, not a generic decay. An annual statistic is not stale for being annual; it becomes stale when a release that was expected does not arrive. Beyond a source-specific hard expiry the indicator drops out entirely.

7. Evidence rules

  • An observation cannot exist without the SHA-256 of the exact bytes it was parsed from.
  • The payload is archived before it is parsed, and parsed from those same bytes.
  • Collectors assert the exact series and dataset identifier; a mismatch fails rather than scoring the wrong series.
  • A blank cell stays blank. It is never read as zero.
  • A unit or definition change quarantines the release for review instead of ingesting it.
  • Observations are append-only: a revision adds a version and records what it superseded.
  • A failed fetch, parse or validation writes nothing; the previous verified value stays and ages.

8. Acute event overlay

News is not sentiment-scored. To affect the score, an event needs a defined scope, measurable operational disruption, corroboration from at least two credible sources, and a named reviewer's approval. Political controversy, commentary and social-media volume do not qualify.

contribution = 2 × (severity ÷ 5) × evidence × reach × breadth × 0.5^(age ÷ half-life)

Each event contributes at most 2 points and the total overlay is capped at 8. The review workflow is not yet built, so nothing currently feeds it.

9. History

There is no historical chart. A reproducible backcast requires acquiring source histories, publishing every transformation and denominator, and marking definition and geography breaks. Until that exists, the history endpoint returns an empty series rather than an illustrative one — a plausible-looking line the model never actually calculated would be the most misleading thing on the site.

10. Change control

Any change to definitions, weights, breakpoints, event rules or missing-data handling requires a methodology version change, updated tests and a published impact note. Prior series must remain reproducible.

11. What this index must never claim

  • That a score proves the UK is about to collapse.
  • That daily recalculation means the inputs are daily.
  • That England-only evidence describes the United Kingdom.
  • That one news story or social-media trend represents the country.
  • That correlation identifies political or institutional causation.
  • That uncertainty can be hidden behind a precise-looking number.
