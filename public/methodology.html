Methodology version 0.1.0

A stress index that shows its workings.

The UK Stability Monitor is a composite measure of national systemic pressure. It is not a forecast of state failure, a political approval score or a substitute for the underlying evidence.

Prototype status. The scoring engine and disclosure model are implemented. The current seed observations remain provisional or illustrative until every value, definition and historical series is independently validated.

1. Headline calculation

Each indicator value is mapped onto a transparent 0–100 pressure scale using published piecewise-linear thresholds. The hard-data score is the weighted mean of available indicators.

base score = Σ(indicator pressure × indicator weight) ÷ Σ(available weights)

An independently reviewed qualitative event overlay may add up to 10 points. It is currently disabled in practice: the prototype includes no event that changes the score.

headline score = clamp(base score + event overlay, 0, 100)

4. Freshness and confidence

The site may recalculate daily, but it never relabels an old statistic as today's figure. Every observation carries its actual reference date, publication date, source and verification status.

Confidence combines source confidence and freshness against the indicator's expected release cadence. Stale data does not automatically reduce or increase the stress score; it reduces confidence in the headline.

5. Event overlay

News events are not sentiment-scored automatically. To affect the index, an event must have a named source, occurrence time, severity, confidence, decay half-life and an approved review state. Each event contributes at most two points before decay, and the total overlay is capped at ten.

event effect = (severity ÷ 5) × 2 × evidence confidence × 0.5^(age ÷ half-life)

6. Historical chart

The first-release history is explicitly labelled an illustrative backcast. It exists to test the interface. It must not be presented as calculated evidence until all ten source series have been acquired, transformed with the same versioned method and independently checked.

7. Change control

Any change to indicator definitions, weights, thresholds, event rules or missing-data handling requires a methodology version change, test updates and a published impact note. Historical values should be regenerated under the new method while the prior series remains reproducible.

8. What this index must never claim

  • That a particular score proves the UK is about to collapse.
  • That daily publication means every input is daily.
  • That one news story or social-media trend represents the country.
  • That correlation identifies political or institutional causation.
  • That uncertainty can be hidden behind a precise-looking number.
