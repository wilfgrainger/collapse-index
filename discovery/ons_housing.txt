Metadata
By expressing monthly rent prices as a percentage of total monthly household income, an indication is provided of the affordability of private renting. We consider an area to have affordable private rent if its rent price is equivalent to or less than 30% of its privately-renting household income. 
More details are available in the Private rental affordability Quality and Methodology Information, or in Section 9 of the accompanying bulletin.
Rent prices
Rents are a mean weighted average, modelled using an internationally recognised process.
Rent prices are rounded to the nearest pound (£).
Private rent prices are republished from our Private rent and house prices, UK series. These rents include a representative mix of new and existing rents in England and Wales. Northern Ireland data are for advertised new lets.We advise users to bear this in mind when interpreting estimates for  and comparing UK countries. 
The estimates in this bulletin are not directly comparable with previous editions. We have  changed the way we compare rents and incomes, using September based rents to best represent a financial year.
Income of private renting households
Incomes are median averages, for the gross monthly income of privately-renting households
Incomes are rounded to the nearest pound (£).
Private renting household income are calculated using data from the Family Resources Survey (FRS), conducted by The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP), Natcen and ONS and accessed via the UKDataService. We filter the FRS to only include those who are privately renting a furnished or unfurnished property, to work out the income of private renters. We use a grossing factor weight to adjust for potential bias in responses to the FRS.
Proportion of income equivalent to rent
To calculate the affordability of private renting, we calculate the percentage of the privately-renting household income that is equivalent to monthly rent for an area. 
Affordability ratios are rounded to the nearest 1 decimal place.
Any year-on-year changes to affordability should be treated with caution, as the private renters' income is derived from a relatively small sample size and so will likely show fluctuations year-on-year, especially at the regional or local level, so we recommend looking at changes over the whole data time series.
Local authority level data, Table 4
To create the local authority level versions of these rents we have scaled the incomes using an additional data source. We used earnings estimates from the Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings (ASHE) series, to scale the FRS incomes. These modelled local authority ratios are official statistics under development, involving additional uncertainty over and above the use of the FRS incomes, and so caution should be used, especially when looking at year-on-year changes.
The Local Authority level estimates are not directly comparable with previous editions. We have changed the source we use to model incomes.
