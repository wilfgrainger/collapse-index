Background information on this dataset
Back to contents
Background
The Trust in Government study aims to monitor people’s trust across different institutions and levels of government. It includes questions on drivers of trust, political attitudes, political participation and satisfaction with public services
The 2023 edition of the study has been conducted in 30 countries who are members of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation Development (OECD), with fieldwork completed in September and October 2023.
The questionnaire was designed by the OECD in collaboration with the Office for National Statistics (ONS) and statistical offices in other participating countries.
The UK data presented in these tables were collected by the Office for National Statistics. More information about the collection and analysis of these data can be found below and in the accompanying bulletin and Quality and Methodology Information article.
Coverage
The UK data in these tables are based on a survey of 1,919 adults (aged 18 years or over) living in the UK. ​
Respondents living in Great Britain were sampled from those who had previously completed the ONS Labour Force Survey. Respondents living in Northern Ireland were sampled from an address file provided by the Northern Ireland Statistics and Research Agency (NISRA).
The survey was conducted between 27 September and 23 October 2023. ONS invited 4,135 people to take part and achieved a response rate of 46% (1,919). 
The survey was conducted online using Smart Survey software. In a small number of cases (1.7%), respondents completed the survey over the telephone with an ONS Telephone Interviewer.
Weighted count
In order to make the sample of a survey representative of the population, responses are weighted. 'Weighted count' provides the representative count for each breakdown. Percentages in this report are based on weighted counts that are representative of the population of adults (aged 18 years or over) living in the UK and are adjusted to address age, sex, regional and educational bias in response rates.
Weighted counts are rounded to the nearest 1,000.
Sample size
This refers to the total number of people who responded to each question. Sample sizes for some response categories may be small and therefore should be treated with caution.
Estimates shaded in light grey relate to statistics based on sample sizes smaller than 30 and should be treated with caution.
Estimates with “[c]" relate to statistics based on small sample sizes (less than 10). Such values have been suppressed on quality grounds and to avoid disclosure issues.
Missing data
There are cases in which respondents did not answer a specific question. Where this happens, they have been excluded from the analysis, which means the sample counts may differ across questions that have the same base population.
Rounding
All estimates in these data tables are individually rounded to whole numbers. Totals may not add exactly due to this rounding. Percentages may not sum to 100% or match figures reported as aggregated categories.
Confidence Intervals
Lower confidence limit and Upper confidence limit refer to Lower and Upper 95% confidence limits. These form a confidence interval, which is a measure of the statistical precision of an estimate and shows the range of uncertainty around the calculated estimate. As a general rule, if the confidence interval around one figure overlaps with the interval around another, we cannot say with certainty that there is more than a chance difference between the two figures.
